Portland and Lake Oswego have long been recognized for offering some of the most exquisite luxury living experiences in the Pacific Northwest. From elegant lakefront estates to private forested retreats just minutes from the city, our region attracts buyers seeking serenity, architectural excellence, and a lifestyle that blends refinement with nature.

One of the first questions I hear from buyers is whether to look in Portland itself or just south in Lake Oswego. They sit side by side, but they are two different places to own a home. Here’s how they compare.

## The Setting: A Big City and a Smaller One

Portland is a large city split by the Willamette River. West of the river, the forested West Hills and Forest Park rise right behind downtown; east of it, older neighborhoods spread out on a street grid with their own commercial main streets.

Lake Oswego is its own city, mostly in Clackamas County, built around Oswego Lake and bounded by the Willamette on the east. It has two main commercial hubs, downtown and Lake Grove, with wooded, hilly residential areas in between. Hillside neighborhoods such as Skylands look out over the valley, with sweeping views of Mt. Hood, the Willamette River, and the rolling hills of the Willamette Valley.

## Housing Stock

**Portland** offers the widest range of home types in the metro:

-   Craftsman bungalows, Foursquares and other early-1900s homes in the older close-in neighborhoods
-   Hillside homes and estates in the West Hills, many built to capture city and mountain views
-   Planned communities such as Forest Heights, with architecture ranging from classic Northwest to contemporary custom homes
-   Lofts, condos and penthouses in the Pearl District and other central neighborhoods

**Lake Oswego** is mostly single-family homes. Expect mid-century ranches, traditional and Northwest contemporary homes, larger custom estates and a steady stream of rebuilds and new construction. Close-in neighborhoods like Lakewood and First Addition feature beautifully restored homes and luxury new constructions, and townhomes and condos cluster near downtown and Lake Grove.

If a condo or a historic city bungalow is on your list, Portland has far more choice. If you want a detached home on a wooded lot, both deliver, and Lake Oswego’s inventory leans heavily that way.

## Commute and Getting Around

Inside Portland, many neighborhoods are served by MAX light rail, the Portland Streetcar or frequent bus lines, and close-in districts like the Pearl and Nob Hill are easy to get around on foot or by bike.

Lake Oswego is more car-oriented. Most commuters drive into downtown Portland on Highway 43 along the river or on I-5, and TriMet buses connect the city to Portland. MAX light rail does not run to Lake Oswego. Downtown Lake Oswego and Lake Grove are walkable once you’re there.

Where you work matters. Commutes vary a lot by time of day, so drive the route at rush hour before you commit to either side.

## Amenities

**Portland** brings big-city amenities: Forest Park and Washington Park, the riverfront, a deep restaurant and food-cart scene, museums, live music and professional sports.

**Lake Oswego** keeps things closer to home: boutique shopping and restaurants downtown and in Lake Grove, the farmers market and summer concerts at Millennium Plaza Park, George Rogers Park on the Willamette, the public Lake Oswego Golf Course and the trails of Tryon Creek State Natural Area on the city’s edge. Portland’s attractions are a short drive away.

## Local Taxes to Check

The two cities do not carry the same local taxes, so ask a tax professional how they apply to your income:

-   Portland residents pay the city’s Arts Tax.
-   Multnomah County residents above an income threshold pay the Preschool for All tax. Most of Lake Oswego is in Clackamas County, though a small part lies in Multnomah County.
-   The Metro Supportive Housing Services income tax applies across the Metro region, which includes both Portland and Lake Oswego, above an income threshold.

Property tax bills also vary by address, so look at the actual tax history of any home you’re considering.

## In Between: Dunthorpe

Nestled between Portland and Lake Oswego, Dunthorpe is one of Oregon’s most iconic luxury enclaves. Known for its sprawling estates, winding tree-lined roads, and unmatched privacy, it offers historic and architect-designed residences on expansive, manicured grounds just minutes from downtown. It has a Portland mailing address but sits outside both cities’ limits, which makes it worth a look if you’re torn between the two.

## Final Thoughts

From the grandeur of Dunthorpe to the scenic serenity of Lake Oswego’s waterfront communities, the Portland metro area offers no shortage of luxury neighborhoods—each with its own unique personality and perks.
