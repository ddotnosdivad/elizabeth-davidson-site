## Portland’s Most Popular Home Styles

### The Classic Craftsman

If Portland had a mascot, it might be the Craftsman home. These charmers date back to the early 1900s and are beloved for their warm woodwork, built-in nooks, and big front porches (perfect for people-watching or your 3rd cup of coffee). Think: wide eaves, exposed rafters, and a whole lot of architectural heart.

Look for low-pitched roofs and exposed beams outside, and inside, original hardwood floors, built-in cabinets and window seats.

### Bungalows (a.k.a. Cozy Central)

Smaller than their Craftsman cousins but just as full of personality, Portland bungalows offer all that charm on a more manageable scale, without the upkeep of a mansion. Bonus: many have been lovingly updated with modern kitchens and eco-friendly touches.

### Victorian & Queen Anne Beauties

Yes, Portland has its fair share of old-school glamour. These ornate homes, often painted in whimsical colors (we see you, Alberta Arts District), bring a splash of fairytale magic to the urban landscape.

Queen Anne homes are known for turrets, gables and intricate woodwork, while Italianate houses show off tall windows and ornate trim under the eaves.

![Victorian Architecture](/assets/img/blog/wix/422cf6621f685161837661409958801b.webp)

### Mid-Century Modern

Cue the Mad Men soundtrack—Portland has *serious* mid-century vibes. Clean lines, big windows, and functional design? Yes, please. You’ll find MCM homes in neighborhoods like Beaverton and the West Hills, often with open floor plans and enough glass to make your houseplants weep with joy.

Built in the decades after World War II, these homes often have low rooflines, post-and-beam construction and walls of glass that open to a deck or garden.

![mid century modern](/assets/img/blog/wix/11062b_a352f344f2bd47b79d9cdbf5bfc4a953.webp)

### Contemporary and Modern

Sleek, bold and energy-efficient, contemporary homes are a growing part of Portland’s housing mix, from new infill houses on narrow city lots to custom homes in the hills (see [new construction luxury homes in Portland](/post/new-construction-luxury-homes-in-portland-what-to-know)). Expect flat or shed roofs, large windows, mixed materials like wood, metal and concrete, and open interiors.

![Contemporary House](/assets/img/blog/wix/11062b_37b2c48d18e34a398b8c8cee99758a23.webp)

### Modern Farmhouse

A newer trend in the Portland metro area, especially in suburbs like Happy Valley and Sherwood, the modern farmhouse blends rustic charm with sleek finishes. Think shiplap, black-framed windows, and open shelving galore.

### Eclectic Finds

Portland also has plenty of homes that don’t fit a single label: A-frames, small cottages and old warehouses converted into lofts. Part of the fun of house hunting here is what turns up around the next corner. To compare areas, see [Portland vs. Lake Oswego](/post/the-most-desirable-luxury-neighborhoods-in-portland-lake-oswego).

### Sustainable Design

Green building is part of Portland’s design culture. You’ll see passive solar design, green roofs, reclaimed materials and high-efficiency systems in new homes and in thoughtful remodels of older ones.

![Portland Architecture](/assets/img/blog/wix/11062b_767974a5d4c844208ab6b14b3075169a.webp)

## Portland Architects Behind Notable Modern Homes

Portland has a strong community of residential architects. A few local firms whose house designs you may come across:

- **Waechter Architecture.** Ben Waechter’s Portland firm is known for spare, carefully detailed modern houses. It was named to *Architectural Record*’s Design Vanguard in 2016 and received the Architectural League of New York’s Emerging Voices award in 2019.
- **Potestio Studio.** Portland architect Rick Potestio designs custom homes that pair classical ideas of proportion and harmony with modern materials.
- **Scott Edwards Architecture.** Founded in Portland in 1998, the firm designs modern houses with flat roofs and walls of glass and has also worked on restorations and additions to historic Portland homes.

## Why Architecture Matters Here

Portland’s architecture is shaped by its landscape, its sustainability culture and its arts community. From a real estate point of view, good design adds real value: buyers notice thoughtful design the moment they walk in, and a well-designed home stands out in any market.
