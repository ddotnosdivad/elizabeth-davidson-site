The holiday season in Portland is a magical time, and one of the highlights is, without a doubt, the annual Christmas tree lighting events that light up the city—and your holiday spirit! Whether you're a Portland native or just visiting, there’s nothing quite like the moment when the Christmas lights first sparkle to life, signaling the start of the most wonderful time of the year.

As a real estate agent who loves showing clients the beauty of [Portland](/blog/city-guides/portland) and its surrounding neighborhoods, I can tell you that our city really knows how to do Christmas! From stunning downtown displays to neighborhood gatherings, here are the top Christmas tree lightings in and around Portland you won’t want to miss.

![Christmas lights in Portland](/assets/img/blog/wix/11062b_fe83b16bf7a24b7fbf2f1830dcee7442.webp)

### 1\. Portland’s Annual Holiday Tree Lighting Ceremony (Downtown Portland)

The heart of Portland's holiday festivities kicks off with the **Annual Tree Lighting Ceremony** in **Pioneer Courthouse Square** (701 SW 6th Ave, Portland), also known as Portland’s "living room." This year’s ceremony, the 42nd, is scheduled for **Friday, November 27, 2026, from 5:30 to 6:45 p.m.**, and it’s a *huge* event that draws thousands of Portlanders. The centerpiece is a 75-foot Douglas fir, adorned with thousands of twinkling lights.

The celebration is a community sing-along with live music and performances, and a festive atmosphere that fills the square. Friends, neighbors and visitors come together to see the tree lit up and enjoy plenty of holiday cheer. It’s a quintessential Portland holiday experience!

**Pro Tip:** Arrive early to grab a good spot, especially if you want to be front and center when the big moment happens!

### 2\. The Grotto’s Christmas Festival of Lights (NE Portland)

If you’re looking for a truly breathtaking holiday experience, you can’t miss **The Grotto’s Christmas Festival of Lights** in Northeast Portland (8840 NE Skidmore St, Portland; the entrance is at NE 85th Ave and Sandy Blvd). This annual event is one of the most stunning Christmas light displays in the city. The Grotto, a Catholic sanctuary set among wooded gardens, transforms into a winter wonderland of lights. The festival features choirs performing in the Chapel of Mary and a serene, peaceful walk through the illuminated gardens. It's perfect for a romantic evening or a quiet outing.

This year the Festival of Lights runs nightly **November 27 through December 30, 2026** (closed Christmas Day), so if you can’t make the opening night, you still have plenty of time to enjoy this Portland gem.

**Pro Tip:** Be sure to wear warm clothing, as it can get chilly, but the beauty of the lights and the peaceful atmosphere are totally worth it.

### 3\. ZooLights at the Oregon Zoo (Washington Park)

The **Oregon Zoo** (4001 SW Canyon Rd, Portland) offers a Christmas lights display that’s fun for all ages! **ZooLights** is a longtime holiday favorite, with a light path of a little over a mile, light tunnels and dozens of life-size animal lanterns. This season it runs **November 20, 2026 through January 3, 2027**, opening nightly at 4:30 p.m. (closed November 24–26 and December 25), and it’s an excellent way to experience the zoo in a completely different way—magical and glowing in the winter night.

You can also hop on the zoo train to see the lanterns along the route, warm up with hot drinks, and enjoy the winter landscapes of Washington Park.

**Pro Tip:** Tickets are sold for specific dates, so buy yours online in advance to save time and avoid the lines!

### 4\. Lake Oswego’s Christmas Tree Lighting (Lake Oswego)

If you’re looking for a charming, small-town vibe with all the big holiday spirit, head to **Lake Oswego** for their annual Tree Lighting Festival at Millennium Plaza Park (200 First St, Lake Oswego). This lovely event takes place in the heart of downtown, where you can enjoy hot cocoa, cookies, and a variety of local entertainment (see the full [Lake Oswego tree lighting guide](/post/christmas-tree-lighting-in-lake-oswego)). The festival is usually held the Friday after Thanksgiving and brings the community together in a festive, intimate atmosphere.

Lake Oswego is known for its picturesque lakefront setting, and during the holidays, it’s especially beautiful with all the lights and decorations reflecting off the water.

**Pro Tip:** If you’re looking to make a whole evening out of it, check out some of the local shops and restaurants around the tree lighting. You can even take a stroll along the lake afterward!

### 5\. Holiday Lights on Peacock Lane (SE Portland)

One of Portland’s most beloved holiday traditions, **Peacock Lane** in Southeast Portland (the block of SE Peacock Ln between SE Stark St and SE Belmont St) is a must-see for anyone in the city during Christmas. This street, lined with beautiful, historic homes, is transformed into a sparkling light display each year. The residents of Peacock Lane go all out, with each house adorning its exterior with thousands of colorful lights, creating a magical experience that draws crowds from all over.

This year the lights are on nightly **December 15–31, 2026**, starting at 6 p.m., and the Lane is pedestrian-only on **December 15 and 16**. Check the Peacock Lane website or Facebook page for closing times and any last-minute changes.

**Pro Tip:** The free cocoa and cider booth runs on donations, and a portion of its proceeds goes to a local charity. Bringing a little cash or a digital donation is a great way to give back while enjoying the magic of the season.

### 6\. Lee Farms in Tualatin

Step into a winter wonderland at Lee Farms (21975 SW 65th Ave, Tualatin), a working farm in Tualatin, Oregon. The farm’s holiday season centers on Christmas trees, and it has hosted a winter festival with lights, holiday music and seasonal activities. Check the Lee Farms website for this year’s holiday dates and events.
