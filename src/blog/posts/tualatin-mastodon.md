Some towns have a statue in the town square. Tualatin has a mastodon in the library. In 1962, Portland State University students dug nearly half of a mastodon skeleton out of a swamp near Nyberg Creek, and today those bones stand inside Tualatin Public Library, in front of an etched-glass mastodon. It's one of my favorite "wait, really?" stops in town, and it's on display whenever the library is open.

![Partial mastodon skeleton mounted in front of an etched-glass mastodon at Tualatin Public Library in Tualatin, Oregon](/assets/img/blog/tualatin-mastodon.webp)

## Quick facts

- **What:** an articulated mastodon skeleton, nearly half of the animal, mounted in front of an etched-glass image of a mastodon
- **Found:** 1962, in a swamp near Nyberg Creek, just south of the current Fred Meyer store parking lot
- **Age:** carbon dating puts its death about 14,000 years ago
- **Species:** the Pacific mastodon (*Mammut pacificus*), identified from detailed measurements of its teeth, and the first reported specimen of that species in Oregon
- **Current display:** created during the library's 2008 renovation and expansion
- **Where:** Tualatin Public Library, 18878 SW Martinazzi Avenue, Tualatin, OR 97062
- **When to see it:** during library hours

## A story that started with the town marshal

The way the library tells it, John George grew up in Tualatin and remembered the town marshal talking about some enormous bones he'd seen in the swamp near Nyberg Creek. In 1962, George, along with Ron and George Sund, spent a month excavating there and recovered nearly half of a skeleton.

I love that this whole discovery traces back to a bit of local storytelling. Someone remembered what an old-timer had said, went looking, and found an Ice Age animal right in town.

## The long road to the library

The bones didn't go straight to Tualatin's library. According to the library, they were first displayed at Portland State University. In the 1970s they were given to Tualatin's city manager at the time, and then spent several years in storage, first in Tualatin and then at the Portland Zoo.

Eventually the Tualatin Historical Society and community members raised money to have the bones cleaned and restored, and the skeleton went on display in the library. The display you see today was created as part of the library's renovation and expansion in 2008.

## What scientists learned

The skeleton was carbon-dated, and the library says the animal died about 14,000 years ago. Scientists estimate this mastodon was a female who died in her 20s.

There's a newer chapter, too. Fossil sites found in California and Colorado led to fresh research on mastodons from western North America and a new species classification: the Pacific mastodon (*Mammut pacificus*). Based on detailed measurements of its teeth, the Tualatin mastodon was identified as a member of that species, which makes it the first reported Pacific mastodon specimen in Oregon. Not bad for a set of bones from a local swamp!

## Seeing it for yourself

The skeleton is inside **Tualatin Public Library, 18878 SW Martinazzi Avenue**. The library's hours are:

- Monday through Thursday, 10 a.m. to 8 p.m.
- Friday through Sunday, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.

A heads-up for the holidays: the library is closed on Thanksgiving Day (it's also closed on New Year's Day, Memorial Day, the 4th of July, Labor Day and Christmas), so plan your visit for another day over the holiday weekend.

For parking, the city's GIS lists three lots at City Offices and the Library: the North lot (59 spaces, 2 accessible), the South lot (21 spaces) and the Back lot (17 spaces, 2 accessible).

While you're there, look for the library's other Ice Age displays. In partnership with the Tualatin Historical Society, the library shows several other Ice Age relics, and there's an interactive mastodon display in the Children's Room. The library also has a story about the Tualatin mastodon, *I Wonder...*, that you can check out.

## Tualatin's Ice Age connections

The mastodon is part of a bigger story. The City of Tualatin has been designated the National Park Service's first partner community along the Ice Age Floods National Geologic Trail, and the library says Tualatin Public Library will serve as the local interpretive center. If you collect Passport to Your National Parks stamps, the library can stamp your book for the Ice Age Floods National Geologic Trail.

The library's Ice Age Floods page and the city's Ibach Park page point to a few more places around town:

- an Ice Age geologic timeline along a segment of the Tualatin River Greenway
- the mastodon bones in the Ibach Park playground
- erratic rocks at the Tualatin Heritage Center
- the proposed Ice Age Tonquin Trail, a planned 22-mile network of bike and pedestrian pathways with interpretive displays about the floods

The mastodon even shows up in public art: in 2015 the city's Community Arts Enhancement Award went to CenterCal's Nyberg Rivers Shopping Center for its "I Wonder" bronze mastodon sculpture.

## Tualatin business of the week

A visit to the library pairs nicely with an easy lunch. A favorite is **Mama's Teriyaki**, serving Japanese dishes featuring soy-glazed meats in a compact counter-serve restaurant. You'll find it at 19210 SW Martinazzi Ave, Tualatin, OR 97062.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I see the Tualatin mastodon?**
Inside Tualatin Public Library, 18878 SW Martinazzi Avenue, where the skeleton is mounted in front of an etched-glass mastodon.

**How old is the Tualatin mastodon?**
Carbon dating puts its death about 14,000 years ago. Scientists estimate it was a female who died in her 20s.

**Where was the mastodon found?**
In a swamp near Nyberg Creek, just south of the current Fred Meyer store parking lot. It was excavated in 1962.

**Is the library open on Thanksgiving?**
No. Tualatin Public Library is closed on Thanksgiving Day. Its regular hours are 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Monday through Thursday and 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Friday through Sunday.
