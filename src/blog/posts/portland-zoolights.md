There's a moment every year when the days get short and I start counting down to ZooLights. The Oregon Zoo trades its daytime routine for wildlife-inspired light displays, glowing animal lanterns and tunnels of twinkling lights, all tucked into the trees of Washington Park. This year's season opens **Friday, November 20, 2026** and runs through **January 3, 2027**, so here's everything I'd want to know before heading up the hill.

## Quick facts

- **Dates:** November 20, 2026 to January 3, 2027
- **Opens:** nightly at 4:30 p.m.; closing times vary by night, from 8:30 p.m. to 10:30 p.m.
- **Closed:** November 24-26 and December 25; January 1-2 are special-event nights with their own admission rules
- **Where:** Oregon Zoo, 4001 SW Canyon Road, Portland, OR 97221, in Washington Park
- **Tickets:** adult prices range from $13 to $35, depending on the night
- **New this year:** 10,000 square feet of heated, covered space at the Wildlife Pavilion

## What you'll see

The zoo describes ZooLights as wildlife-inspired light displays with plenty of seasonal fun. The zoo train is a highlight: you ride past dozens of life-size animal lanterns along the route. On foot, you can walk through vibrant tunnels of glittering lights and soak up the glow.

![Zoo train locomotive outlined in red, blue and green holiday lights at night at the Oregon Zoo in Portland, Oregon](/assets/img/blog/portland-zoolights-2.webp)

*Photo: Fuzzykitten27 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0 ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WP%26ZRy_Steam_1.jpg))*

One thing to know before you go: the zoo notes that most animals are not on view during ZooLights. This is a night for the lights, so plan a daytime visit if you're hoping to see the animals themselves.

And the treats! The zoo lists elephant ears, hot chocolate, popcorn and s'mores, plus beer, wine and a range of alcohol-free drinks. Food and drink stands are spread across the grounds, from Cascade Cafe at the entry plaza to Central Plaza, Elephant Lands, Discovery Plaza and the Wildlife Pavilion, and the zoo says their hours vary by location.

That new heated, covered space at the Wildlife Pavilion is a welcome addition for those classic damp December nights, and a nice spot to warm your hands around a hot chocolate before heading back out.

## Dates, hours and closed nights

ZooLights opens at 4:30 p.m. every night it runs, but closing times change through the season. Some nights close at 8:30 p.m., and many December nights run as late as 10 or 10:30 p.m. A few dates to circle:

- **Value Nights:** November 20-23, November 30-December 3, and January 3
- **Closed:** November 24-26 and December 25
- **January 1 and 2:** special-event nights with their own admission rules, so check the zoo's page before planning one of those
- **Last night:** Sunday, January 3, 2027

Because hours shift night to night, I always check the zoo's calendar for my date before I go.

## Tickets

The zoo says prices and times vary through the season, with adult tickets ranging from **$13 to $35**. Zoo members receive 20% off regularly priced tickets, with no additional discount on Value Nights.

If you're shopping for someone who loves holiday lights, the zoo also sells **ZooLights Any Night** tickets. You buy them in advance and use them on any night from November 20 to December 31, 2026, which makes them an easy gift to tuck into a card.

## Getting there and parking

The zoo sits in Washington Park, just west of downtown Portland. A few options:

- **Drive and park:** there's a limited amount of parking in the lot next to the zoo and throughout the park. You pay at pay stations with coin or card, or through the Parking Kitty app. All of it is managed by Portland Parks & Recreation.
- **Park & Ride:** the zoo points to the Sunset Transit Center, about 8 minutes and two exits west, with 600+ free parking spaces. From there, you ride the MAX one stop east to the zoo, and the zoo says trains arrive every 10 minutes or less.
- **MAX light rail:** the Red and Blue lines stop at the underground Washington Park station, where elevators take you within a few steps of the zoo entrance.

With parking limited, I love the MAX option, and here's the bonus: the zoo says your ZooLights ticket is your "ticket to ride" TriMet (bus, MAX, WES and LIFT) and the Portland Streetcar to and from the zoo on your admission date. It isn't valid on C-TRAN. No circling for a spot, and the station drops you right by the entrance.

## A little Washington Park history

Portland Parks & Recreation calls Washington Park "the crown jewel of Portland." It's a 410-acre park that's also home to the World Forestry Center, Hoyt Arboretum, the International Rose Test Garden and the Portland Japanese Garden, and it draws 3.5 million visitors a year.

The zoo has deep roots here. According to the city, Portland's first zoo began in the mid-1880s, when Dr. Richard B. Knight exhibited animals on a lot next to his downtown pharmacy. By 1887 the collection had outgrown its home, and he donated the animals to the city. The zoo moved within the park in 1925, to the site of today's Japanese Garden, and opened at its present location in 1959.

So when you're riding the train past glowing lanterns, you're part of a story that goes back well over a century.

## Portland business of the week

After a chilly night under the lights, there's nothing like settling in somewhere warm. A favorite is **Fireside**, a striking gastropub with dual fireplaces serving New American fare, wine and cocktails. You'll find it at 801 NW 23rd Ave, Portland, OR 97210.
