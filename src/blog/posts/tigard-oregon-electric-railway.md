Picture Tigard in 1907, a farming community with a general store and a post office called Tigardville. Then, on January 1, 1908, passenger trains on the Oregon Electric Railway started rolling through town, about a dozen of them a day. The little depot is long gone, but Main Street grew up around it, and you can still trace that story on a walk downtown today.

## Quick facts

- **Passenger service began:** January 1, 1908, shortly after the last spike was driven near downtown Tigard
- **Route:** between Portland and Salem
- **Trains through Tigard:** about a dozen a day
- **The depot:** a small station that sold tickets to passengers; it has since been razed
- **Where it stood:** today's Rotary Plaza and Tigard Outdoor Museum on Main Street
- **See it today:** Rotary Plaza, open daily from dawn to dusk, where the Tigard Heritage Trail begins

## The last spike and the first trains

According to the Tigard Public Library's Main Street History Tour, passenger service on the Oregon Electric Railway began January 1, 1908, shortly after the last spike was driven near downtown Tigard, completing the line between Portland and Salem. The railway built a small depot here that sold tickets to passengers.

The tour says about a dozen trains went through Tigard daily, letting residents reach downtown Portland quickly and comfortably for the first time. It wasn't just people riding, either. Local dairy farmers used the railway to send large containers of milk to market in Portland.

The railway even touched the town's name. As I shared in my post on [how Tigard got its name](/post/tigard-name-origin), a Tigard Life history article says Tigardville became plain Tigard around 1907-08, when the Oregon Electric was built through town.

## How the railway built Main Street

The History Tour says the Oregon Electric transformed Tigard's early growth. It attracted entrepreneurs who helped set up a business district near the station, and real estate investors who bought land around Tigard and began subdividing tracts for future home buyers.

You can follow it year by year in the tour:

- **1908:** Tigard's Main Street post office was established after the Tigardville Post Office was dissolved. Adolf Schamoni's Germania Hall (12370 SW Main St.) is considered the first building put up on Main Street after the Oregon Electric began service.
- **1909:** Harry Kuehne moved a wood-frame building across Main Street and ran a livery stable there. He rented horses and carriages to salespeople who arrived in Tigard by train and needed a way to reach the surrounding farms.
- **1911:** Hugh Butler Tigard built the H.B. Tigard General Store on Main Street. He'd been running a store in Portland and moved back when the railway was built; the tour notes that Oregon Electric officials encouraged entrepreneurs to build general stores close to town train stations, hoping the population would follow. The store closed in December 1945 after 34 years.

The City of Tigard tells the story with a slightly different date: its downtown page says Main Street's identity as Tigard's center dates from the construction of an electric commuter train line in 1910, followed by rapid growth with restaurants, grocery stores, a dance hall, a blacksmith and a livery stable.

## When the highway changed things

The trains made Main Street, and the highway later reshaped it: the city says a Pacific Highway (99W) overpass went in just north of Main Street in 1940, and new development moved toward Pacific Highway. That's a story for another day.

## Where to see the railway story today

**Rotary Plaza, 12345 SW Main St.** The History Tour places the razed Tigard station at today's Rotary Plaza and Outdoor Museum. The city describes the plaza as a gathering space in the middle of downtown that connects to the Heritage Trail, and it's open daily from dawn to dusk.

**The Tigard Heritage Trail.** The city's Heritage Trail follows a three-quarter-mile inactive rail corridor from Rotary Plaza toward Tiedeman Avenue, with a railroad-themed Outdoor Museum along the way. It's a story of its own, and I'll save the details for another post.

**Main Street itself.** Bring the library's History Tour along and match the addresses as you walk. I wrote more about today's downtown in my [Downtown Tigard guide](/post/tigard-downtown-tigard), and you'll find all my Tigard posts on my [Tigard city guide page](/blog/city-guides/tigard).

## Tigard business of the week

For a railway story, I couldn't resist a train-themed stop. A favorite is **Pizza Caboose**, a pizzeria offering varied pies, casual eats and a salad bar in a train-themed interior. You'll find it at 11670 SW Pacific Hwy, Tigard, OR 97223.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did the Oregon Electric Railway come to Tigard?**
Passenger service began on New Year's Day, 1908, according to the Tigard Public Library's Main Street History Tour.

**Where was Tigard's train depot?**
The tour places the razed Oregon Electric station at today's Rotary Plaza and Outdoor Museum on Main Street.

**How many trains ran through Tigard?**
The History Tour says about a dozen went through town daily on the Portland-Salem line.

**Is there an old rail corridor downtown I can walk?**
Yes. The Tigard Heritage Trail follows a three-quarter-mile inactive rail corridor between Tiedeman Avenue and Main Street.
